# LINK & BUILD — Game Design Document

*A satirical incremental game about engagement farming on LinkedIn.*

---

## 1. Vision

A web game that is indistinguishable from LinkedIn — the real UI, the real feed, the
real notification bell, the real "Open to Work" banner, the real Premium upsell popup —
except it isn't LinkedIn. It's a game. The only tell is that the numbers go up in a way
that feels *satisfying*.

The player starts as a nobody writing posts by hand, learns the unwritten rules of
engagement farming, and climbs through the real-world industry: engagement pods,
outsourced likes from cheap commenters, growth agencies, and finally a fully automated
AI factory that pumps out meaningless "thought leadership" at infinite scale.

The whole time, the game brags about **corporate productivity** — "streamline your
workflow," "scale your personal brand," "40 hours saved a week" — while the player is
producing nothing of use. The game rewards you for being pathetic. That complicity is
the point.

The player should finish a session and think: *"Wait, was that... fun? Was I just doing
LinkedIn?"*

---

## 2. The Core Fantasy

**LinkedIn is already an incremental game.** You post, farm impressions, chase likes,
level up your "thought leader" status, and prestige by going viral. This game just makes
the hidden mechanics explicit.

The player is not watching the joke — **they are doing it, and the game is laughing at
them for doing it.**

---

## 3. The Feed

An infinite scroll of recognizable LinkedIn archetypes — the Gym Bro CEO, the
Humbled-to-Announce Guy, the AI Slop Poster, the URGENT Recruiter, the MLM Hun, the the
Burnout-Poster posting from a yacht. The feed never ends. The next post might be the
viral one.

---

## 4. Writing & Posting

The player writes and posts their own content, choosing from the same tired formats
everyone uses — humble-brags, hot takes, mental-health-from-a-yacht, posts, AI
mototivational quotes, carousels, polls. Every choice is a small act of engagement bait.

---

## 5. Watching the Numbers Grow

When a post goes live, the player watches its impressions climb, likes pop in one by one,
and comments arrive. A little economy happening live in the feed. Milestones flash up:
"100! 500! 1,000!"

---

## 6. The Notification Bell

The red dot that never stops. "Someone viewed your profile." "New connection request."
"Your post reached 500 impressions." The player is trained to keep checking the bell.
That's the loop.

---

## 7. The Automation Ladder

From writing every post by hand, to joining an engagement pod, to scheduling and
analytics, to buying cheap outsourced likes from commenters with broken English, to a
growth agency running networks of fake accounts, to a fully automated AI factory that
writes, comments, and replies on its own.

Every tier is sold in glowing corporate language — "streamline your workflow," "scale
your personal brand" — while the content gets more meaningless.

---

## 8. The Productivity Satire

The game brags about productivity while doing nothing of use. "I automated my entire
workflow. 40 hours saved a week." The player's character sits on a yacht while an AI
writes "In the symphony of business, resilience is the crescendo 🎶."

You're braaging about corporate productivity while actually doing nothing of use. The
game rewards you for it.

---

## 9. The Risk

Push the farm too hard and the algorithm catches you. The feed goes quiet. A warning
appears. Your account gets flagged. The game feels genuinely unsettling when it happens.

---

## 10. The Idle Gut-Punch

"While you were away, your post gained  ‎4,,432 impressions." Returning to a big number
feels good — and it makes the satire land, because you feel *pride* in fake internet
points.

---

## 11. Tone

- The UI is 100% LinkedIn: nav bar, left rail, "Open to Work" banner, Premium upsell
  popup, notification bell with red dots
- The only tell is that the numbers go up in a way that feels satisfying
- The game occasionally breaks the fourth wall: a post that says *"You've been scrolling
  for 2 hours. Your real boss is wondering where you are."*
- The player should finish a session and realize they've been playing this game for real
  the whole time.

---

## 12. What "Winning" Feels Like

You don't win by escaping LinkedIn. You win by **becoming** the thing you mocked — a
fully automated, meaningless thought leader with a verified badge, a yacht, and a feed
of AI slop that people can't stop engaging with. The victory screen is a humble-brag
post that writes itself.

*The satire is the game. The game is the satire.*
