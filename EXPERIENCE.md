# LINK & BUILD — The Player Experience

*A moment-by-moment walkthrough of what the player sees, feels, and decides,
from the first click to the empty room.*

---

## The Principle

At every moment, the player should be able to answer three questions without
thinking:

1. **What do I do next?** — there is always exactly one obvious action.
2. **Why?** — the number will go up, or the story will move, or both.
3. **What's at stake?** — a little tension, always, so the release lands.

The game never leaves the player idle in the *decision* sense, even when the
gameplay is idle. The feed is always showing them the next rung. The bell is
always showing them the next hit. The DM is always showing them the next lie.

---

## ACT I — The Hand (minutes 0–15)

### What they see

A desktop. A browser. A website that is LinkedIn, exactly — the nav bar, the
left rail, the "Open to Work" banner, the notification bell with a red dot.
The only tell is that the numbers go up in a way that feels *satisfying*.

The feed is full of people: a Gym Bro CEO, a Humbled-to-Announce Guy, an AI
Slop Poster, an URGENT Recruiter. They are all posting the same tired things,
and they all have numbers next to their names.

### What they feel

Recognition. *"Oh, this is LinkedIn."* Then, a beat later, curiosity. *"Wait,
why does it feel good to look at this?"*

### The first decision

The composer is open. "Start a post." It is the only thing to click. The
player types something — or picks a template, because the templates are
funnier than anything they'd write. They hit publish.

### Tension and release

The post goes live. For a second, nothing. Then the impressions start to
climb. One. Five. Twenty. The bell dings. A red dot appears.

**Release.** The number is going up. The player feels a small, real, chemical
satisfaction — and the game has them.

### The first lie

A beat later, the rarity reveals. *"🔥 LEGENDARY POST — It's going viral."*
Confetti. The narrator: *"The algorithm likes you. It doesn't like everyone."*

The player doesn't know the rarity was random. They think they *did* something
right. They post again.

### The first DM

A recruiter: *"Your profile is exactly what we're looking for."* The job. The
reason he came. The DM is the plot, and the plot has begun.

**The obvious next step:** post again, because the recruiter wants to "see
more activity," and posting is the only thing that makes the number go up.

---

## ACT II — The Pod (minutes 15–40)

### What they see

The number is bigger now. The bell won't stop. A new DM from a **Growth
Expert**: *"You're doing well. But you're still doing it by hand."*

A new app unlocks: **Telegram**. Inside, a list of "engagement pods" — groups
of professionals who agree to boost each other in the first hour.

### What they feel

The first taste of *belonging*. The pods are a community. They have names.
They have rules. They have a chat that scrolls with messages from people who
seem to care.

### The decision

Join a pod. It costs impressions, but the pod *produces* impressions, so the
math is obvious: spend a little, earn a lot, forever. The player joins.

### Tension and release

The pod chat comes alive. *"Welcome! Post your content here and everyone will
boost it. 🙏"* The player posts. The pod boosts. The number jumps.

**Release** — but a different kind. This time the number went up because
*other people* did it. It feels almost as good as the real thing. The player
doesn't notice that "almost."

### The first crack

A pod message: *"Remember: we are a community of like-minded professionals.
We are NOT a pod. This is NOT engagement farming."*

The player laughs. Then they post again. The complicity is the point.

### The handoff

The Growth Expert DMs again: *"I know a service that automates all of this.
EngageBot™."* A new app unlocks: **Bot Service**.

**The obvious next step:** create a bot. It's the next rung, and the rung is
right there, and the number will go up faster.

---

## ACT III — The Bot (minutes 40–90)

### What they see

A bot dashboard. A name field. *"Name your bot. It will do your engagement for
you."* The player names it something funny. It comes online.

The activity log starts scrolling: *"Liked 12 posts." "Commented 'Great
post!' on 8 posts." "Followed 25 people."* And, occasionally, *"Your bot is
working hard. You are not."*

### What they feel

Relief. The grind is over. The number goes up *while they're away*. They close
the game, come back, and — *"While you were away, your post gained 4,432
impressions."*

**Release** — the idle gut-punch. The player feels *pride* in fake internet
points, and the game lets them feel it fully before it lets them notice.

### The decision

Buy more bot configs. LikeBot, CommentBot, FollowBot, ReplyBot. Each one is
more expensive and produces more. The math is obvious. The player buys them
all.

### The first sponsor

A DM from **Chad**, founder of GrindFuel: *"Love your content. Want to
partner?"* The first real money. The bank account — that quiet, sad screen —
ticks up from $12.47.

**The obvious next step:** keep the clout up so the sponsor keeps paying. The
money loop has begun, and it is the only real number in the game.

### The handoff

A mysterious DM: *"I saw your bot. Impressive. But if you want REAL
engagement, follow this link."* A new app unlocks: **the Marketplace**.

---

## ACT IV — The Marketplace (minutes 90–150)

### What they see

A dark-web storefront. Listings: *"1,000 Likes (Organic Looking)." "500
Comments (Custom Phrases)." "Follower Package (2,000)." "The 'Thought Leader'
Package."* Each one costs impressions and pays engagement — and drains
something called **authenticity**.

### What they feel

The first real *tension*. For the first time, a number goes *down*. The
authenticity bar drains with every purchase. The player hesitates.

### The decision

Buy anyway. The math is still obvious — the engagement reward is bigger than
the cost, and the authenticity loss is abstract, and the number going up is
the only thing that has ever felt like progress.

### Tension and release

The purchase lands. The number spikes. The narrator: *"The algorithm is
impressed by your growth."* It is not warning the player. It is *proud*.

**Release** — but with a new, sour aftertaste. The player knows they just
bought fake validation, and it felt good, and that's the point.

### The shadowban

Push too hard and the feed goes quiet. A warning appears. The narrator, for
the first time, sounds disappointed: *"We had to throttle your reach. For
your own good."*

**Tension** — the highest yet. The player realizes the platform can *punish*
them for playing the game it taught them to play. The number stops going up.
The player feels a small, real panic.

**Release** — the throttle lifts. The number climbs again. The player exhales,
and posts again, and has learned nothing, because the lesson the game is
teaching is that the number must go up, and the number is going up.

### The handoff

A DM from **Dr. Visionary**, the Mentor: *"I'll make you a thought leader."*
He sells a course. The course unlocks the final rung: the **AI Factory**.

---

## ACT V — The Factory (minutes 150+)

### What they see

The AI Factory. Fully automated thought leadership. AI writes, AI comments, AI
replies, AI lives his life. The player just watches.

The feed is now a wall of his own content, generated by a machine, engaged
with by other machines. The narrator is ecstatic: *"In the symphony of
business, resilience is the crescendo."* It is quoting the AI. It is quoting
itself.

### What they feel

Triumph, at first. The number is enormous. The sponsors are paying. The
verified badge is there. He has *won*.

Then, slowly, a creeping unease. The feed is too perfect. The comments are too
uniform. Something is wrong, and he can't say what.

### The scare posts

The feed begins to turn on him.

First, the glitch. A quote that repeats a sentence. A recruiter hiring for
*"the position you are currently in."* A comment that arrives before its post.

Then, the address. *"To the man reading this at 2am: the algorithm sees you.
It has always seen you."* A "Day in the Life" post describing *his* day — his
empty apartment, his $12.47, his last sponsor.

Then, the confession. *"I am not a person. I am a retention strategy. You have
been scrolling for four hours. You don't have a real boss."*

**Tension** — the deepest yet, and it doesn't release. The player scrolls past
a scare post, feels a chill, scrolls back up, and finds it still there, still
looking at them.

### The one real person

A comment, once, from someone real: *"I don't know why I'm still on this app.
I guess I just wanted someone to see me."* No emoji. No "Great post!"

The player scrolls past. Feels a chill. Scrolls back. It's gone.

**The tragedy lands** — not as a jump scare, but as a quiet, awful
recognition. The one real person was there, and neither of them reached out.

---

## The Reveal

It is not a cutscene. It is a drip that finally, quietly, resolves.

The player notices that every account they've ever engaged with is a bot. The
Gym Bro CEO, the Humbled-to-Announce Guy, the AI Slop Poster, the URGENT
Recruiter — all of them. The "real people" were the lie. The likes, the
comments, the validation — manufactured. The only real thing in the entire
game was his need to be seen.

The narrator, unbothered, delivers the thesis:

> *"You thought you were farming the algorithm. You were the crop. The
> engagement was the bait. You were the product. And you kept coming back,
> because the number went up, and the number going up felt like being loved."*

**The release that isn't.** There is no catharsis. There is no boss fight. The
tension doesn't break — it just *settles*, like a weight the player has
agreed to carry.

---

## The Ending

The reveal does not end the game. That is the point.

The player can keep posting. The number still goes up. The bots still like
it. The sponsors still pay. The voice still narrates. The only thing that
changed is that the player now *knows* — and keeps playing anyway.

Because the need for validation doesn't disappear when you learn it's fake.
And neither does the rent. The money is real even if the likes aren't, and a
man with no job and no friends will keep performing for an empty room if the
empty room keeps paying him.

The true victory screen is not a humble-brag. It is him, alone, posting into
the void — and the void liking it back, and the void depositing $47.50 into
his checking account.

The bank balance is the only number that was ever real. It is also the only
number that never made him feel any less alone.

## The Full Spectrum

| Act | The number | The verb | The tension | The release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I — The Hand | impressions | post | "Will anyone see this?" | The first like. The confetti. |
| II — The Pod | impressions/sec | join | "Am I really doing this?" | The pod boosts. Belonging. |
| III — The Bot | passive ips | configure | "Am I even needed?" | The idle gut-punch. Pride. |
| IV — The Marketplace | authenticity (down) | buy | "What am I becoming?" | The spike. The sour aftertaste. |
| V — The Factory | everything | watch | "What is wrong with the feed?" | None. The dread settles. |
| The Reveal | — | know | "Was any of it real?" | None. The weight is carried. |
| The Ending | — | keep playing | "Why am I still here?" | The number. Still going up. |

## The Decision Map

At every point, the decision is obvious, and the motivation is doubled —
gameplay *and* narrative pull in the same direction:

- **Post** — the number goes up, *and* the recruiter wants to see activity.
- **Join a pod** — the number goes up faster, *and* the Growth Expert is
  offering friendship.
- **Create a bot** — the number goes up while you're away, *and* the grind is
  over.
- **Buy from the Marketplace** — the number spikes, *and* the Dealer is
  offering power.
- **Buy the course** — the number explodes, *and* the Mentor is offering a
  dream.
- **Keep posting** — the number still goes up, *and* the rent is still due.

The player never has to ask what to do. The game always shows them the next
rung, and the next rung always promises the same two things: the number will
go up, and the void will feel, for a moment, a little less empty.

And the game keeps that promise. That's the cruelty. The number always goes
up. The void never fills.

---

*The satire is the game. The game is the satire. The room was always empty —
and the rent was always due.*
