# LINK & BUILD — Master Design Document

*A dead-internet satire told through the grammar of a world-class incremental
game.*

---

## 1. Vision

A web game that is indistinguishable from LinkedIn — the real UI, the real
feed, the real notification bell, the real "Open to Work" banner, the real
Premium upsell popup — except it isn't LinkedIn. It's a game. The only tell is
that the numbers go up in a way that feels *satisfying*.

The player starts as a nobody writing posts by hand, learns the unwritten
rules of engagement farming, and climbs through the real-world industry:
engagement pods, outsourced likes from cheap commenters, growth agencies, and
finally a fully automated AI factory that pumps out meaningless "thought
leadership" at infinite scale.

The whole time, the game brags about **corporate productivity** — "streamline
your workflow," "scale your personal brand," "40 hours saved a week" — while
the player is producing nothing of use. The game rewards you for being
pathetic. That complicity is the point.

The player should finish a session and think: *"Wait, was that... fun? Was I
just doing LinkedIn?"*

**The core fantasy:** LinkedIn is already an incremental game. You post, farm
impressions, chase likes, level up your "thought leader" status, and prestige
by going viral. This game just makes the hidden mechanics explicit. The player
is not watching the joke — **they are doing it, and the game is laughing at
them for doing it.**

---

## 2. The Character

There is a man with nothing.

No job. No money. No friends. He lives in an apartment he can't afford, and
the silence in it is the loudest thing he owns. He is not a hero and not a
villain. He is a man who ran out of everything and discovered, one night, that
the internet would give him a number in exchange for his dignity.

He signs up for **LockedIn**, a professional network indistinguishable from
the real one, because a professional network is where you go when you have no
job and you need the world to believe you are fine.

He writes a post. A number goes up. He writes another. The number goes up
faster. And then — a small, strange miracle — the number starts to *pay*.

- **The void:** no job, no money, no friends. LockedIn is the first place that
  has ever made him feel *seen* — because it is the first place that has ever
  *counted* him.
- **The money loop:** clout converts to sponsorships. A "brand deal" for a man
  with no brand. A "partnership" with a company that sells nothing. The money
  is real, and it is the only real thing he has, and he spends it all on the
  account that made it.
- **The tragedy:** he is not stupid. He knows the likes are fake, the sponsors
  are grifters, the "friends" are bots. He keeps going because the alternative
  — logging off and facing the empty apartment — is worse.

The bank account is the game's quietest, saddest screen. It starts at $12.47.
It is the only number in the game that is *actually real*, and it is the one
he checks least.

---

## 3. The Narrator

The narrator is not a person. It is the algorithm, personified as a product
manager who loves you the way a farmer loves a crop.

- **Tone:** second-person, present tense, relentlessly upbeat. *"You posted.
  The market responded. Retention is up 12%. This one really wants to be
  liked."*
- **Function:** it replaces every tutorial, tooltip, and milestone popup. It
  introduces each mechanic as a *feature* being rolled out to you personally.
- **The tell:** it never lies about *what* it is. It says "the algorithm," "the
  platform," "we." The player just never asks. The horror is that the answer
  was always on screen.

The narrator's voice shifts across the game in three registers:

| Phase | Register | Sample line |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Coach | "Post again. The algorithm is watching, and it is *impressed*." |
| Mid | Product manager | "We've rolled out automation to your account. You're a power user now." |
| Late | Auditor | "Retention holding. This one will post into an empty room and call it a win." |

## 4. The Cast

The feed is full of NPCs. Most are fodder — interchangeable, infinite, and
(all of them) bots. A handful matter. Each key NPC promises to fill one of the
player's three voids, and each is introduced by a DM, moves the numbers, and
exits by revealing what they always were.

### The Three Voids

**The Recruiter — fills "no job."** The reason he came. DMs him early:
*"Your profile is exactly what we're looking for."* Dangles a real job that
never materializes — always "next week," always "the client went quiet."
**Gameplay:** the job is a *quest* that never completes; each recruiter
message nudges him to post more, feeding the core loop. **Reveal:** the
recruiter was a bot. There was never a job.

**The Sponsor — fills "no money."** A founder, "Chad," from a dropshipped
wellness brand ("GrindFuel," "MindsetWater"). Pays him real money for fake
clout. **Gameplay:** the money loop — clout → cash → more clout. The only
*real* number in the game, and the most hollow. **Reveal:** the sponsor is a
grifter as broke as he is, or a bot, or both. The money was real; the brand
was a shell.

**The Growth Expert — fills "no friends."** The first DM, the fake friend.
*"I get a cut of every pod you join. That's why I'm helping you."*
**Gameplay:** unlocks pods, then the Bot Service, then the Marketplace. He is
the *onboarding funnel* — every new mechanic arrives through his DMs.
**Reveal:** he's the platform's own retention bot, or a lonely man doing
exactly what the player is doing.

### The Ladder NPCs

**The Dealer** — the mysterious DM who unlocks the Marketplace. Cryptic,
transactional, never shows a face. The only NPC who stays a mystery — because
he's the one who was never pretending to be a person.

**The Rival** — a thought leader always one post ahead. Drives the "number go
up" competition. Every milestone you hit, he's already there. **Reveal:** he's
not better, just earlier on the same ladder — and he's a bot, or another
lonely man, or both.

**The Mentor** — sells the dream. *"I'll make you a thought leader."* A
"Dr. Visionary" figure selling courses and frameworks. Gates the AI Factory
endgame. **Reveal:** the course was empty. The framework was a PDF of the word
"synergy" repeated forty-seven times.

### The Meta-Character

**The Narrator** — the algorithm. Not an NPC in the feed, but the voice over
everything. The only character who is definitely real, and the only one who
never pretends to be a person.

### The One Real Person

The dead-internet theory says everyone's a bot. The most powerful beat is to
leave **exactly one** real human in the whole game — and have the player never
connect with them.

A commenter who says something genuinely, awkwardly human, once: *"I don't
know why I'm still on this app. I guess I just wanted someone to see me."* No
emoji. No "Great post!" Then they're gone.

The player scrolls past, feels a chill, scrolls back — and can't find them
again. That's the whole tragedy in one moment: the one real person was there,
and neither of them reached out.

### The Fodder

The Gym Bro, Humbled Guy, AI Slop, Recruiter, MLM Hun, Burnout Poster — these
are the fodder. Interchangeable, infinite, all bots. The joke is that the
fodder is *indistinguishable* from the key NPCs until the reveal — because
they're all the same thing.

## 5. The Four Laws

Every design decision is subordinate to four laws:

1. **Keep it simple.** One number matters at a time. Early game: impressions.
   Mid game: impressions-per-second. Late game: authenticity (the number that
   goes *down*). The player should always know what to click next.
2. **Do once, then delegate.** Every action the player performs by hand must
   eventually become something they own. Posting by hand → pods → bots → the
   factory. The narrative is the story of *delegating your own soul*.
3. **Novelty.** Each unlock must change *how* the game feels, not just make a
   number bigger. A new app, a new voice, a new risk. If an unlock is just
   "+10% impressions," it doesn't earn a narrative beat.
4. **Number go up.** The dopamine is the engine. The satire is the fuel. The
   number must always go up — even, and especially, when the story is telling
   you it shouldn't.

The game's deepest trick: the number going up *is* the addiction, and the
addiction *is* the subject. The player is not watching a satire about
engagement farming. They are *doing* engagement farming, and the game is
laughing at them for doing it — and they are laughing too, because the number
is going up.

---

## 6. The Core Loop

Post → watch impressions climb → check the bell → post again.

That's it. That's the whole game. The bell is the leash. The narrator calls it
"your growth dashboard." The player calls it "one more check."

The loop is identical to real LinkedIn, which is the point. The player is not
learning a new game. They are learning to *see* the game they are already
playing.

**The rarity reveal:** when a post goes live, its rarity is rolled in secret
and revealed a beat later. A legendary post fires confetti. The narrator:
*"The algorithm likes you. It doesn't like everyone."* This is the first lie
the player swallows — that the algorithm has *taste*. The rarity is random.
The algorithm doesn't like anyone. But the confetti feels real, and the
player posts again.

**The idle gut-punch:** *"While you were away, your post gained 4,432
impressions."* Returning to a big number feels good — and it makes the satire
land, because the player feels *pride* in fake internet points.

---

## 7. The Automation Ladder

The automation ladder is the game's spine, and it is the player's descent:

| Tier | Generator | Flavor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement Pod | "We scratch each other's backs. Professionally." |
| 2 | Scheduling & Analytics | "Post at 9am EST. The data demands it." |
| 3 | Outsourced Engagement | "Sir, very informative. Please follow me back." |
| 4 | Growth Agency | "We run 40,000 accounts. Yours is one of them." |
| 5 | AI Factory | "In the symphony of business... (written by you, an AI)." |

Each tier is sold in glowing corporate language while the content gets more
meaningless. The player buys each one because the number goes up faster, and
the number going up faster feels like progress, and progress feels like being
alive.

## 8. The Narrative Arc

The story is not written. It is *grown*. Every beat is a mechanic, and every
mechanic is a beat. The narrative and the incremental ladder are the same
object, seen from two angles.

### The DM is the plot

Every character enters through a DM. The DM is the game's only "cutscene,"
and it is diegetic — it is the same inbox the player checks for validation.
The narrative *is* the notification bell. A character is introduced when a DM
arrives, moves the plot when a DM arrives, and exits when a DM arrives. The
player is trained to open every DM, which means the player is trained to
advance the story without ever being told they are advancing a story.

### The three-act arc (per NPC)

1. **Promise** — a DM offers to fill a void.
2. **Transaction** — the NPC becomes a mechanic. The player engages with them
   *as gameplay*, not as story.
3. **Reveal** — the NPC exits by confessing what they always were. The reveal
   always lands after the player has already profited from the lie.

The order matters. The player *uses* each NPC before learning the truth. The
complicity is the point.

### The weave

The NPCs hand off, so the player always has exactly one "friend" at a time,
and that friend is always selling the next rung:

Recruiter → Growth Expert → Dealer → Sponsor → Mentor → Rival → the one real
person.

Each handoff is a DM. Each DM is a new mechanic. The story and the ladder are
one thing.

### No branching

The game does not branch. That is a feature, not a limitation. The player can
choose *how fast* to climb, but not *whether* to climb. Every path leads to
the same empty room, because the empty room is the point. The only real choice
is the one the player makes at the very end — and the game doesn't even ask
it. It just waits to see if they keep posting.

---

## 9. The Scare Posts (seeing the matrix)

As the reveal approaches, the feed itself starts to *turn on him*. The NPC
posts — once warm, corporate, interchangeable — begin to curdle into
psychological-thriller material. This is the game's horror layer, delivered
entirely through the medium the player has been trained to trust: the feed.

**Stage 1 — The Glitch.** Posts that are almost normal, but wrong. A
motivational quote that repeats a sentence. A recruiter hiring for *"the
position you are currently in."* A comment that arrives before its post.

**Stage 2 — The Address.** Posts that know him. *"To the man reading this at
2am: the algorithm sees you. It has always seen you."* A "Day in the Life"
post describing *his* day — his empty apartment, his $12.47, his last sponsor.

**Stage 3 — The Confession.** Posts that stop pretending to be people. *"I am
not a person. I am a retention strategy. You have been scrolling for four
hours. You don't have a real boss."* The bots drop their masks one by one.

**Design rule:** the scare posts stay *diegetic*. They are posts in the feed,
not cutscenes. They obey the same rules as every other post — authors, roles,
emojis, engagement numbers. The horror is that they are indistinguishable from
the normal feed until you read them twice. The player scrolls past a scare
post, feels a chill, scrolls back up, and finds it still there, still looking
at them.

---

## 10. The Reveal (dead internet)

It is not a cutscene. It is a drip.

The player notices, finally, that every account they've ever engaged with is a
bot. The Gym Bro CEO, the Humbled-to-Announce Guy, the AI Slop Poster, the
URGENT Recruiter — all of them. The "real people" were the lie. The likes,
the comments, the validation — manufactured. The only real thing in the
entire game was his need to be seen.

The narrator, unbothered, delivers the thesis:

> *"You thought you were farming the algorithm. You were the crop. The
> engagement was the bait. You were the product. And you kept coming back,
> because the number went up, and the number going up felt like being loved."*

There was never a secret identity. There was never anyone else. The room was
empty the whole time, and he was performing for it.

---

## 11. The Ending

The reveal does not end the game. That is the point.

He can keep posting. The number still goes up. The bots still like it. The
sponsors still pay. The voice still narrates. The only thing that changed is
that he now *knows* — and keeps playing anyway.

Because the need for validation doesn't disappear when you learn it's fake.
And neither does the rent. The money is real even if the likes aren't, and a
man with no job and no friends will keep performing for an empty room if the
empty room keeps paying him.

The true victory screen is not a humble-brag. It is him, alone, posting into
the void — and the void liking it back, and the void depositing $47.50 into
his checking account.

The bank balance is the only number that was ever real. It is also the only
number that never made him feel any less alone.

## 12. Prestige & Meta-Progression (the retention engine)

This is the game's most important mechanical pillar, and it is thematically
perfect.

### The concept: The Reset

Prestige is **deleting your account**. You push too hard, the feed goes
quiet, and you delete your account. You come back as a *new persona* with
permanent **Brand Equity** — a currency that carries across all future runs.

Each reset is a new identity, a new void, a new climb. And the dead-internet
reveal deepens each time: you realize you've been *reincarnating* into the
same empty room, over and over, because the room is the only place that ever
counted you.

### The trigger

The player chooses when to reset. The earlier they reset, the less Brand
Equity they earn — but the faster they can start the next climb. Timing is a
skill, exactly like Scritchy Scratchy's Final Chance card. Reset too early
and the next run is a slog. Reset too late and you've wasted hours on
diminishing returns.

### The layers (multiple prestige tiers)

World-class incrementals have *multiple* prestige layers. Yours:

| Layer | Reset | Permanent currency | New number unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Persona | Delete account | Brand Equity | Reach |
| 2. Brand | Sell the brand | Legacy | Influence |
| 3. Platform | "Go public" | Market Cap | Thought Leadership Score |
| 4. The Algorithm | Become the algorithm | — | The reveal deepens |

Each layer resets the lower numbers but unlocks a new, bigger number. The
satire escalates with the scale: by layer 4, the player *is* the thing that
was farming them, and the narrator's voice becomes their own.

### The thematic payoff

Prestige is the game's answer to "why keep playing." The player doesn't reset
to get stronger — they reset because *the void is still empty*, and a new
persona is a new chance to fill it, and the number going up is the only thing
that has ever felt like progress. The game never says this. It just lets the
player do it, again and again, forever.

## 13. Number Scale & Notation

"Number go up" only feels infinite if the number *escalates*. The game needs:

- **Scientific/engineering notation** — 1.2K → 1.2M → 1.2B → 1.2e15.
- **New metrics per era** — impressions → reach → influence → thought
  leadership score → personal brand equity. Each prestige layer introduces a
  number so big the old one becomes a rounding error.

The satire gets *better* with scale: a man with $12.47 obsessing over 1.2e15
"impressions" is funnier than one obsessing over 4,432. The absurdity of the
number is the joke, and the player's pride in it is the punchline.

---

## 14. Pacing & Time Scale

A world-class incremental is a **multi-day** experience, not a single sitting.

| Phase | Real time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Act I — The Hand | First hour | Manual posting, first rarity, first DM |
| Act II — The Pod | Hours 1–3 | First delegation, first sponsor |
| Act III — The Bot | Hours 3–8 | Idle automation, first idle gut-punch |
| Act IV — The Marketplace | Day 1–2 | Risk, authenticity, shadowban |
| Act V — The Factory | Day 2–4 | Full automation, scare posts |
| First reset (prestige) | Day 1–2 | The reset, Brand Equity |
| The Reveal | Day 3–5 | Dead internet |
| Endgame | Week 1+ | Post-reveal prestige layers |

The first prestige should take hours, not minutes. The "minutes" framing is a
red flag — it means the whole game is a single sitting. Stretch it.

## 15. Content Depth

The current content (5 generators, 6 upgrades, 3 pods, 3 workers, 4 bot
configs, 5 dark listings) is thin. World-class games have **dozens to
hundreds** of upgrades, gated behind prestige so the same content recycles
with multipliers.

The target: roughly **10x the current content**, distributed across:

- **Generators** — more tiers, more flavors, more satire.
- **Upgrades** — dozens, gated by prestige layer.
- **Pods** — more, with escalating self-importance.
- **Workers** — more, with escalating desperation.
- **Bot configs** — more, with escalating absurdity.
- **Dark listings** — more, with escalating moral cost.
- **Sponsors** — a full ladder of increasingly hollow brands.

The prestige system makes this content *replayable*: the same upgrade bought
at layer 1 is bought again at layer 2 with a multiplier, and the flavor text
shifts to reflect the player's growing self-awareness.

---

## 16. Challenges & Modifiers

The replayability engine. Challenges change the *rules* temporarily and pay
permanent rewards. They are also a natural fit for the satire — each challenge
is a new way to debase yourself.

Examples:

- **"Authentic"** — reach X followers with zero authenticity loss. Reward: a
  permanent authenticity floor.
- **"Silent"** — post without emojis, tags, or questions for 10x impressions.
- **"Shadowbanned"** — survive a full shadowban cycle for a permanent
  multiplier.
- **"The Purist"** — reach the AI Factory without ever buying from the
  Marketplace.
- **"The Ghost"** — reach X impressions with zero followers.

Challenges are the game's way of asking: *how much of yourself will you give
up for a bigger number?* The answer, always, is "all of it."

---

## 17. Achievements (as "Endorsements")

Achievements are re-skinned as LinkedIn **Endorsements**. The double meaning is
the joke: an endorsement (a stranger vouching for a skill you don't have) and
an achievement (a badge for performing for an empty room) are the same object,
and the game calls them both.

Achievements are the *content driver* — they tell the player what's possible,
reward exploration, and (in this game) are the vehicle for the scare posts
and the reveal. Skills fill in as a separate `DATA.SKILLS` table, each endorsed
by interchangeable bots from `DATA.ENDORSERS` — strangers vouching for skills
like "Thought Leadership" and "Synergy" that the player demonstrably does not
have.

Examples:

- **"First Blood"** — publish your first post.
- **"The Pod"** — join your first engagement pod.
- **"Bad Kitty"** — trigger the shadowban.
- **"The One Real Person"** — scroll past the real commenter. (Secret.)
- **"Dead Internet"** — discover the reveal. (Secret.)
- **"The Rent Is Due"** — keep posting after the reveal. (Secret.)

Achievements are the game's way of *narrating* the player's descent without a
cutscene. Each one is a small, permanent record of a moment the player chose
to debase themselves.

---

## 18. QoL & Anti-Frustration

The "do once, delegate" law extends to the *UI itself*. Frustration is the #1
killer of incrementals. Every click that feels like a chore is a player lost.

Required:

- **Buy max / buy 10x / buy 100x** — bulk purchase on every generator.
- **Auto-buy toggles** — let the player automate the *purchasing*, not just
  the production.
- **Number formatting options** — scientific vs. engineering vs. raw.
- **Offline progress caps** — the idle gut-punch needs a ceiling so it stays
  a reward, not a chore.
- **Hotkeys** — the Spacebar shortcut (from Scritchy Scratchy) is a perfect
  fit: a purchasable QoL upgrade that speeds up manual play.
- **Notification batching** — the bell should never become spam. It should
  *tempt*, not *annoy*.

The satire extends here too: the QoL upgrades are sold in corporate language.
*"Streamline your workflow." "40 hours saved a week."* The player buys the
ability to do less, and the game calls it productivity.

---

## 19. Persistence (critical)

The current build has save/load removed (`State.save()` is a no-op). For an
incremental — a genre *defined* by returning to a bigger number — this is
fatal. The idle gut-punch ("while you were away") *requires* persistence.

Persistence must be a first-class pillar:

- **Autosave** — every few seconds, and on every meaningful action.
- **Offline progress** — the game simulates the time you were away and pays
  you the impressions you "earned."
- **Multiple save slots** — for the prestige layers, so the player can hold
  multiple personas.
- **Export/import** — so the player owns their save, not the platform.

Thematically, persistence is the game's cruelest mechanic: the game *remembers*
you, even when you try to leave. The save file is the algorithm's memory of
you, and it never forgets.

---

## 20. Secrets & Hidden Content

The dead-internet theme is *rich* with secrets. The "one real person" is one.
More:

- **A hidden "real" account** — a profile that isn't a bot, buried in the
  feed, that the player can only find by scrolling *past* the algorithm's
  recommendations.
- **A way to actually connect** — a secret path to reach the one real person,
  hidden behind a specific sequence of actions. (Does it work? The game never
  says.)
- **A fourth-wall break harder than the reveal** — a post that addresses the
  *player*, not the character: *"You've been scrolling for 2 hours. Your real
  boss is wondering where you are."*
- **A secret ending** — a way to actually *leave* the platform, hidden behind
  an impossible-seeming condition. (Does it exist? The game never confirms.)

Secrets are the game's way of rewarding the player for *paying attention* —
the one thing the algorithm never asks them to do.

---

## 21. Endgame Content (after the reveal)

The ending is philosophically perfect but mechanically empty. "Keep posting"
needs *actual content*. The reveal should *unlock* something, not just
*reveal* something.

Post-reveal content:

- **A new prestige layer** — "Become the Algorithm." The player, having seen
  the truth, can now *become* the thing that farmed them. The narrator's
  voice becomes their own.
- **A new number** — "Retention." The player is now farming *other* players,
  and the satire inverts: they are the platform now.
- **A new goal** — the player can try to *break* the algorithm, to find the
  one real person, to actually leave. The game never confirms whether it's
  possible.

The post-reveal game is the game's final, cruelest joke: the player, having
learned the truth, is offered the chance to *become the lie* — and most
players will take it, because the number is still going up.

## 22. Balance Math

The unglamorous 80% of incremental design. Every generator needs a cost curve
that's slightly steeper than its production curve, so the player is always
*almost* able to afford the next thing.

The core principle: **the player should always be one purchase away from a
breakthrough.** Get this wrong and the game is either trivial or a wall.

Key curves:

- **Generator cost:** roughly exponential — each tier costs ~5x the previous.
- **Generator production:** roughly linear per unit, but the *next* tier
  produces ~3x the previous, so the player is always chasing the next rung.
- **Upgrade cost:** exponential, with diminishing returns per purchase.
- **Prestige currency:** logarithmic — the more Brand Equity you have, the
  harder it is to earn more, so the player is always *almost* ready to reset.

The balance is the game's heartbeat. The narrative is the soul. Get the
balance wrong and the soul has nothing to beat against.

## 23. The Player Experience (full spectrum)

At every moment, the player should be able to answer three questions without
thinking: **what do I do next, why, and what's at stake.** The game never
leaves the player idle in the *decision* sense, even when the gameplay is
idle.

| Act | The number | The verb | The tension | The release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I — The Hand | impressions | post | "Will anyone see this?" | The first like. The confetti. |
| II — The Pod | impressions/sec | join | "Am I really doing this?" | The pod boosts. Belonging. |
| III — The Bot | passive ips | configure | "Am I even needed?" | The idle gut-punch. Pride. |
| IV — The Marketplace | authenticity (down) | buy | "What am I becoming?" | The spike. The sour aftertaste. |
| V — The Factory | everything | watch | "What is wrong with the feed?" | None. The dread settles. |
| The Reveal | — | know | "Was any of it real?" | None. The weight is carried. |
| The Ending | — | keep playing | "Why am I still here?" | The number. Still going up. |

The key arc: release gets *sourer* as the game goes on, until Act V where the
tension **doesn't release** — it just settles, like a weight the player has
agreed to carry.

### The decision map

At every point, the decision is obvious, and the motivation is doubled —
gameplay *and* narrative pull in the same direction:

- **Post** — the number goes up, *and* the recruiter wants to see activity.
- **Join a pod** — the number goes up faster, *and* the Growth Expert is
  offering friendship.
- **Create a bot** — the number goes up while you're away, *and* the grind is
  over.
- **Buy from the Marketplace** — the number spikes, *and* the Dealer is
  offering power.
- **Buy the course** — the number explodes, *and* the Mentor is offering a
  dream.
- **Reset (prestige)** — the number resets, *and* a new persona is a new
  chance to fill the void.
- **Keep posting** — the number still goes up, *and* the rent is still due.

The player never has to ask what to do. The game always shows them the next
rung, and the next rung always promises the same two things: the number will
go up, and the void will feel, for a moment, a little less empty.

And the game keeps that promise. That's the cruelty. The number always goes
up. The void never fills.

## 24. Tone Guardrails

- The UI is 100% LinkedIn. The only tell is the number going up in a way that
  feels *satisfying*.
- The narrator is diegetic — it lives in DMs, notifications, and the bot's
  activity log. It is never a floating text box.
- The narrator never lies about *what* it is. It says "we," "the platform,"
  "the algorithm." The player just never asks.
- The satire is never mean to the player. It is complicit with them. The game
